01 / QUANTUM INFORMATION

Algorithms, information
and inference.

In Quantum Information and Computation, I studied algorithms including Shor’s, Grover’s and Deutsch–Jozsa, along with quantum circuits, oracle models and complexity. I earned 85% in the course.

I’m especially interested in quantum algorithms for optimisation and inference, and in how they might work under the constraints of near-term and fault-tolerant devices. I also explored the mathematical link between classical tomography and quantum state tomography: both are reconstruction problems, where a state has to be estimated from measurement data.

02 / IMAGE RECONSTRUCTION

Learning from
incomplete data.

My first-semester master’s project was on computed tomography image reconstruction. I worked with discretised Radon transforms and methods including Tikhonov regularisation and primal–dual hybrid gradient optimisation.

I liked the challenge of recovering an image from measurements that are indirect, noisy or incomplete. I then looked independently at how the same mathematical structure appears in quantum state tomography, and I’d like to explore quantum reconstruction methods further.

03 / QUANTUM COMPUTING IN ASTRONOMY

What does the hardware
need to work?

My second-semester master’s project looks at a quantum-computing platform for astronomy, using a superconducting-qubit architecture. Alongside studying Josephson junctions, transmon and SQUID devices, I’ve been learning how the cryogenic system constrains the experiment.

Our work has included looking at the cooling chain—from pulse-tube precooling through sorption cooling to dilution refrigeration—and modelling its thermal behaviour. We have also analysed temperature data from the apparatus and considered practical bottlenecks such as vacuum quality, wiring thermalisation and parasitic heat leaks.

The project has made the gap between an idealised model and a working device tangible to me. I want to keep building hands-on experience in quantum hardware and experimental measurement.

The planned next step is to develop a cryogenic test system for characterising a small number of qubits at temperatures approaching 50 mK, and to investigate applications in astronomy and cosmology. Measurements would include qubit frequency, anharmonicity, coherence times, coupling strengths and charge sensitivity.
