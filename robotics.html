01 / THE PROBLEM THAT INTERESTS ME

Finding a gas leak
with a mobile robot.

The RAINZ CDT project that interests me is autonomous mobile detection and localisation of gas leaks in industrial facilities. It brings together the areas I want to learn more about: sensors, mathematical modelling, estimation, navigation and decision-making.

It also has a clear practical purpose. As energy systems change, monitoring critical infrastructure safely and reliably matters. I’m interested in how an autonomous system can gather measurements in a challenging environment and use them to work out where a leak is coming from.

02 / A CONNECTION TO MY MASTER’S PROJECT

Reconstructing
what you can’t see.

For my first-semester master’s project, supervised by Professor Sean Holman, I worked on computed tomography image reconstruction. I studied analytical reconstruction, discretised Radon transforms, Tikhonov regularisation and optimisation methods, including the primal–dual hybrid gradient algorithm.

The part I enjoyed most was trying to recover hidden information from noisy, incomplete measurements. I see a useful parallel with gas-source localisation: in both cases, you have to infer something you cannot observe directly from the measurements you can collect.
